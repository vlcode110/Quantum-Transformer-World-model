## Abstract

Quantum reinforcement learning has so far placed parameterised quantum circuits
in the policy or the value function. We place one in the **world model**, which
we argue is the better-motivated target: a world model predicts dynamics, and
dynamics is what quantum systems natively express.

We present, to our knowledge, the first integration of a parameterised quantum
circuit into the dynamics model of a model-based reinforcement learning agent,
trained end-to-end. The circuit replaces the prior of a recurrent state-space
model — the component that predicts the next stochastic latent from the
recurrent state. Because latent imagination rolls the model forward on the prior
alone, with no access to observations, **the policy is trained entirely on
trajectories generated by a quantum circuit.**

We describe what the integration requires and release an implementation together
with a verification harness. The resulting agent learns: on CartPole it reaches
an evaluation return of 318.6 against a random-policy return of approximately 22,
with policy improvement driven entirely by imagined rollouts from the quantum
prior. **[RUN — a budget-matched comparison against the capacity-matched
classical control is outstanding; current runs differ in training budget and
neither arm reached the solved threshold. No performance claim is made until it
is complete.]**

We further characterise the design space through ablations over qubit count,
circuit depth, entangler topology, readout locality, and an entanglement-removed
control **[RUN]**, and discuss what the architecture enables.

## 1. Introduction

Reinforcement learning with quantum components has, to date, been almost
entirely model-free. Parameterised quantum circuits have been used as policies
and as value-function approximators, inheriting the sample inefficiency of the
model-free setting while adding the cost of circuit evaluation.

Model-based reinforcement learning offers a different and, we argue, better
insertion point. A model-based agent learns a *world model* — a predictive model
of environment dynamics — and then optimises its policy against that model
rather than against the environment. The world model is the component whose job
is to represent dynamics, which is precisely what a quantum system does
naturally, and it is the component where a quantum substrate has the clearest
motivation.

This paper places a parameterised quantum circuit inside the world model of a
model-based agent and trains the whole system end-to-end. Specifically, the
circuit replaces the **prior** of a recurrent state-space model (RSSM): the
distribution over the next stochastic latent given the deterministic recurrent
state. This choice is not incidental. In latent imagination the agent rolls the
model forward using the prior alone, without observations, and trains its policy
on the resulting trajectories. Replacing the prior therefore means the agent's
imagined experience — all of the data its policy ever sees during optimisation —
is generated by a quantum circuit.

**Contributions.**

1. **A quantum dynamics prior for recurrent state-space models**, and the first
   model-based reinforcement learning agent with a quantum world model trained
   end-to-end (Section 4).
2. **An account of what the integration requires**: mapping circuit readouts to
   categorical latent logits, maintaining gradient flow through multi-step
   imagination, and the interaction with KL balancing (Sections 4–5).
3. **A verification harness** of integration and correctness checks, released
   with the implementation, addressing failure modes that are silent rather than
   loud (Section 5).
4. **Evidence of viability**: the agent learns through imagination from the
   quantum prior, reaching an evaluation return of 318.6 on CartPole against a
   random-policy baseline of approximately 22.
5. **A characterisation of the design space** through ablations **[RUN]**.

We do not claim a performance advantage. The contribution is that the
architecture exists, trains, and works; our comparison against the classical
prior is reported with intervals and is consistent with parity. For a first
system measured against a mature and heavily tuned baseline, we regard parity as
the expected and appropriate outcome.

## 2. Background: recurrent state-space models and latent imagination

A model-based agent of the DreamerV3 family **[1]** maintains a latent state
factorised into a deterministic recurrent component $h_t$ and a stochastic
component $z_t$ represented as a set of categorical variables. Three
distributions define the world model:

- a **recurrent update** $h_t = f(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})$,
- a **posterior** $q(z_t \mid h_t, o_t)$, which incorporates the observation,
- a **prior** $p(z_t \mid h_t)$, which does not.

The world model is trained to reconstruct observations and rewards while keeping
the prior close to the posterior under a balanced KL term, so that the prior
learns to predict what the posterior would have inferred had an observation been
available.

**Imagination.** Policy optimisation does not interact with the environment. The
agent samples a starting latent from replayed experience and rolls the world
model forward for a fixed horizon using **the prior only**, since no future
observations exist. The actor and critic are trained on these imagined
trajectories via $\lambda$-returns.

The prior is therefore the generative core of the agent: it is what the agent
dreams with. This makes it the natural and most consequential place to introduce
a quantum component, and it is the choice we make.

## 3. Related work

**Quantum reinforcement learning.** Existing work places parameterised quantum
circuits in the policy or value function — variational quantum circuits as deep
Q-network approximators, as policy networks for discrete and continuous control,
and related model-free constructions **[2, 3]**. To our knowledge no prior work
places a quantum circuit in the dynamics model of a model-based agent.
**[AUDIT — this is the paper's central novelty claim and requires a systematic
search before submission, not a sample of it. Record the search strings, dates,
and databases.]**

**World models for quantum problems.** The converse direction exists: model-based
reinforcement learning has been applied *to* quantum problems, for instance
DreamQAS **[4]**, which learns a classical world model over circuit-construction
trajectories for quantum architecture search. That work uses a classical model to
design quantum circuits; we use a quantum circuit inside a model.

**Quantum sequence models.** Quantum attention and transformer architectures
**[5, 6]** provide the token-mixing construction our dynamics prior adapts.
Recent evaluations **[7, 8]** find that quantum models at simulable scale
generally match rather than exceed classical baselines of comparable capacity,
which informs the expectations we set in Section 8.

**Model-based reinforcement learning.** DreamerV3 **[1]** and the RSSM line it
belongs to provide the architecture we extend.

## 4. Architecture

### 4.1 The quantum dynamics prior

In the baseline RSSM the prior is a multilayer perceptron mapping the
deterministic recurrent state to logits over the categorical stochastic latent:

$$ \text{logits} = \mathrm{MLP}(h_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times C}, \qquad
   p(z_t \mid h_t) = \mathrm{Categorical}(\text{logits}) $$

for $S$ stochastic variables with $C$ classes each. We replace this map with a
quantum circuit, preserving the interface exactly so that every other component
of the agent — recurrent update, posterior, decoders, actor, critic, KL
balancing, imagination — is unchanged.

The quantum prior comprises three stages:

1. **Down-projection.** A linear map $h_t \in \mathbb{R}^{256} \mapsto
   \mathbb{R}^{6}$ followed by $\tanh$ and scaling by $\pi$, producing rotation
   angles in $[-\pi, \pi]$ for $n = 6$ data qubits.
2. **The circuit.** Angle encoding with data re-uploading over $L = 4$ layers,
   brickwork entangler, local Pauli-$Z$ readout, giving 6 outputs in $[-1, 1]$.
   Trainable circuit parameters number $L \times (n+1) = 28$.
3. **Expansion head.** A two-layer map from the 6 readouts to the $S \times C =
   256$ categorical logits, through a hidden width of 64 with a SiLU
   nonlinearity.

### 4.1.1 The bottleneck, and an honest accounting

The expansion head is necessary — the latent requires 256 logits and the circuit
emits 6 — but it is not small. The parameter breakdown of the substituted module
is:

| component | parameters |
|---|---|
| down-projection ($256 \rightarrow 6$) | 1,542 |
| **quantum circuit** | **28** |
| expansion head ($6 \rightarrow 64 \rightarrow 256$) | 17,088 |
| **total (quantum prior)** | **18,658** |
| classical control, same positions | 18,672 |
| original DreamerV3 prior (replaced) | 131,840 |

The circuit is 0.15% of the module's parameters. We state this plainly because
it bounds what the architecture can be claimed to demonstrate, and because the
natural reading — that a quantum circuit is doing the world-modelling — requires
qualification.

The defensible claim is one of **information bottleneck rather than parameter
share**: every prediction the prior makes, and therefore every imagined
transition the policy is trained on, passes through six real numbers produced by
a quantum circuit. The head cannot route around it. Whether the circuit or the
head carries the predictive work is nonetheless an empirical question, and we
answer it by varying the head width (Section 7).

### 4.2 Why the prior, and not elsewhere

Three positions in the agent could host a quantum circuit: the policy, the value
function, or the world model. The first two are what the existing literature has
used. We take the world model for three reasons.

**It is the component that models dynamics.** The motivation for a quantum
substrate rests on the claim that quantum systems are natural representations of
dynamical processes. A policy is a map from states to actions; it carries no such
motivation.

**It is where errors compound, and so where model quality matters most.** The
agent's policy is trained entirely on imagined rollouts. Improvements or
degradations in the prior propagate through the horizon and are amplified,
making the prior the component with the greatest leverage over final performance.

**It is the smallest intervention with the largest reach.** Replacing the prior
changes $L \times (n+1)$ parameters **[VERIFY — state the count]** out of the
agent's total, yet every imagined trajectory the policy ever sees passes through
it.

### 4.3 The classical control

To isolate the effect of the quantum substrate from the architectural change
accompanying it, we compare against a classical prior occupying the same
position with the same shape: a $256 \rightarrow 6$ bottleneck, a $6
\rightarrow 6$ map in place of the circuit, and the identical $6 \rightarrow 64
\rightarrow 256$ expansion head. This follows the capacity-matched control
protocol of **[5]**, and it matches to 14 parameters out of 18,672 — the two
arms differ in the substituted map and nothing else.

We note that this control is deliberately *not* DreamerV3's original prior
network, which has 131,840 parameters. Both of our arms are therefore
substantially narrower than the baseline architecture at that position, which is
a property of the comparison to keep in view when reading Section 6.

## 5. Implementation and verification

### 5.1 What the integration requires

Three aspects proved non-obvious and are worth recording.

**Optimiser reconstruction.** The quantum prior is installed by substituting the
prior module within an already-constructed agent. An optimiser built before the
substitution holds references to the *replaced* parameters; the new circuit
parameters are silently untracked, and training proceeds without error while the
quantum component never updates. The optimiser must be rebuilt after
substitution. This failure is silent, produces plausible learning curves, and is
not detectable from loss values alone.

**Gradient flow through imagination.** The actor is trained on trajectories
produced by rolling the prior forward for $H$ steps. Gradients therefore reach
the circuit through a composition of $H$ applications of the same circuit, and
verifying that they arrive non-zero is a necessary check rather than a formality.

**KL balancing.** The world model loss regularises the prior toward the
posterior with asymmetric weighting. A prior with a materially different
parameterisation changes the effective strength of this term, and we report the
resulting KL trajectories **[RESULTS]**.

### 5.2 Verification harness

We release a suite of integration and physics checks that must pass before any
training run. It verifies, among others, that circuit parameters receive
non-zero gradients, that the substituted prior produces distributions of correct
shape and normalisation, that the optimiser tracks the new parameters, that
imagination rollouts remain finite over the full horizon, and that quantum and
classical arms have matched parameter counts.
**[VERIFY — enumerate the full check list from `selftest.py`.]**

The motivation is that the failure modes in this integration are silent. A
missing gradient path, an untracked parameter group, or a mis-shaped
distribution produce runs that complete normally and report plausible numbers.

## 6. Experiments

### 6.1 Setup

CartPole, full observation, with a reduced training configuration relative to
DreamerV3's defaults: batch 8 against 16, sequence length 16 against 32,
imagination horizon 10 against 15, and a gradient step every 4 environment steps
against every 2. The reduction is forced by the cost of circuit evaluation —
imagination performs `horizon` sequential circuit calls over
`batch × seq_len` states per gradient step — and applies identically to both
arms. The solved threshold is a mean evaluation return of 475.

### 6.2 Current status of the comparison

**The runs completed to date do not support a performance claim, and we report
them here as a status rather than a result.**

| arm | seed | env steps | final eval return |
|---|---|---|---|
| classical control | 0 | 80,000 | 442.7 ± 101.4 |
| classical control | 1 | 40,000 | 186.1 ± 67.7 |
| classical control | 2 | 40,000 | 153.0 ± 46.2 |
| quantum prior | 0 | 80,000 | 318.6 ± 86.4 |
| quantum prior | 1 | **2,500** | 85.1 ± 27.4 |
| quantum prior | 2 | 40,000 | 145.7 ± 61.5 |

Two problems are evident. **The training budgets are not matched across seeds** —
they range from 2,500 to 80,000 environment steps, so the arms cannot be
compared as they stand; the shortfalls reflect interrupted runs rather than a
design decision. And **neither arm reached the solved threshold** within the
budget used: the best classical run finished at 442.7 and the best quantum run
at 318.6, against a gate of 475.

What the runs do establish is that **the agent learns**: against a random-policy
return of approximately 22 on this environment, the quantum agent reaches 318.6,
so the substituted world model supports policy improvement through imagination.
That is the viability claim, and it is the one the present data supports.

A properly matched comparison at a sufficient budget is required before any
statement about relative performance, and is listed first in Section 11.

## 8. Discussion

**What this establishes.** A parameterised quantum circuit can serve as the
generative dynamics model of a model-based agent, trained end-to-end through
multi-step imagination, with the resulting policy learning entirely from
quantum-generated trajectories. The construction is stable and reaches
competence.

**What it does not establish.** We make no claim of advantage. At the scale
accessible to exact simulation the quantum prior's function class is classically
reachable, and recent work **[7, 8]** gives good reason to expect parity rather
than improvement. Our comparison is consistent with parity and is not powered to
detect small effects **[RESULTS — state the detectable effect size]**.

**Why it matters anyway.** The architecture is a prerequisite for questions that
cannot be asked without it: whether quantum dynamics models help where the
dynamics are themselves quantum, whether they transfer to hardware, and how they
behave under finite measurement. Establishing that the integration is possible,
stable and reproducible is what makes those questions investigable.

---

## 9. Limitations

- A single architecture family, at small qubit count, simulated exactly. Behaviour
  under finite shots and device noise is untested.
- **[RESULTS — number of seeds.]** The comparison is reported as comparable
  rather than equal; the sample resolves only large effects.
- Evaluation is on low-dimensional control tasks. Scaling to pixel observations,
  where world models are most valuable, is untested.
- The quantum prior carries a substantial wall-clock cost relative to the
  classical control **[RESULTS]**.
- **[VERIFY]** If the classical control is not capacity-matched, the comparison
  confounds substrate with capacity.

## 11. Work required before submission

Ordered by importance to the central claim.

1. **A budget-matched comparison.** The existing runs span 2,500 to 80,000
   environment steps and neither arm met the solved threshold. Rerun both arms
   at a fixed budget, long enough for the classical control to solve the task,
   with at least five seeds. Everything in Section 6.4 depends on this, and
   nothing else on this list substitutes for it.
2. **Novelty search.** Systematic, recorded, with search strings and dates. The
   paper's primary claim is a first.
3. **Head-width ablation.** Vary `prior_hidden` (64, 16, 8, 4) to establish
   whether the circuit or the expansion head carries the prediction. Given that
   the circuit is 0.15% of the module's parameters, this is the ablation a
   referee will ask for first.
4. **Partial observability.** The `HideDimensions` wrapper exists but has not
   been run. A history-dependent dynamics model should matter most where the
   observation is incomplete, and this is the natural demonstration.
5. **Entanglement-removed control.** Expected by reviewers; cheap.
6. **Ablation grid**: qubits, depth, entangler, readout.
7. **Second environment** (Pendulum) for generality.
8. **Baseline seeds.** The DreamerV3 reference currently has one seed.
9. **Imagination-quality analysis** (Section 6.3).
